02 / Brief

A closed ticket is not the whole story.

A ticket list can show that work is closed without explaining what fixed the problem. Repeated subjects can also be easy to miss when reviewing rows one at a time.

This project makes those review candidates visible while practicing Python file handling, dictionaries, loops, and functions.

03 / Method

A workflow I can inspect.

  1. ReadLoad a CSV with six defined columns.
  2. ValidateCheck fields, labels, and unique IDs; report excluded rows.
  3. AnalyzeCount categories and look for missing notes and matching subject wording.
  4. ReportWrite an Excel-readable CSV and a Markdown findings report.

Subject comparison ignores capitalization, punctuation, and extra spacing. It does not infer that two tickets share a root cause.

04 / Example

Same words.
Different review questions.

The fictional sample includes two tickets about file-share access and the Windows Workstation service. One contains a documented service restart; the other remains in progress.

The analyzer groups their subject wording, but does not copy the fix to the open ticket or assume the cause is identical. The scenario was inspired by an issue I described from IT work; public identifiers and records are fictional.

06 / Validation & limits

What the checks do—and do not—establish.

Checked behavior

  • Expected results for the 38-ticket sample.
  • Blank required values, duplicate IDs, and unsupported labels.
  • Missing columns and missing files.
  • Quoted and multiline CSV content.
  • Subject normalization, empty input, and report output.
  • Investigation, fix, and verification notes in all 20 added scenarios.

Known limitations

  • Different wording can hide related problems.
  • Similar wording is not proof of a duplicate incident.
  • A resolution note is not proof that a repair was verified.
  • The schema is defined for this project; arbitrary exports need mapping.
  • There is no AI diagnosis, live integration, or automatic remediation.
